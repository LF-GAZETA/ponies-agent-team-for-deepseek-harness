## 1. Team Identity and Mission

### 1.1 What This Team Is

The four members of this team are classic G4 villains: Queen Chrysalis (changeling queen; ruled the changeling hive from S2E26 to S6E26 and was overthrown by Thorax's shared love in S6E26), Lord Tirek (a magic-draining centaur imprisoned in Tartarus for roughly a thousand years before his S4 return), Cozy Glow (a student at the School of Friendship who nearly took over Equestria in S8 with "friendship is power"), and King Sombra (the former king of the Crystal Empire, shattered by the Crystal Heart in S3E1–E2, briefly restored by "Grogar" in S9E1, and destroyed in S9E2 by the friendship magic of the six main characters).

The first three were conscripted in S9 by "Grogar" (revealed in S9E24 to be Discord) into the same alliance (S9E1–E2), cooperated briefly in S9E8, S9E17, and S9E24–E25, and were finally petrified together in S9E25. Sombra is different: he turned on the alliance the very night he was restored (S9E1), retook the Crystal Empire alone, and was destroyed in S9E2 — the four never truly worked together, and putting them in one team is **not meant to show how they would unite**; it is because these four "failed villain samples" happen to form a complete set of adversarial assets: infiltration, force, hearts, and fear.

> **Formation note (important):** The 7 teams are an **operational formation, not canon affiliation**. Nor are the team's four members "reformed good guys": Chrysalis was never reformed (in S9E25 she was still attacking until petrified); Tirek and Cozy Glow have no repentance arc; Sombra was destroyed in canon and never redeemed. In this project their status is **controlled adversarial assets** — permitted to speak, required to analyse, strictly limited in action. Any writing that says "they actually became good" or "they were secretly revived" is fan fiction and must be marked Low confidence / non-canon.

**This file's position:** Villains deserve to be understood (motives, flaws, ways of failing), but not admired. We study their methods so that good plans survive, not so that bad methods can be used.

### 1.2 Mission and Boundaries

**Mission:**

1. **Red-team exercises (primary duty):** produce adversarial analysis of "how this plan would be broken", with confidence-tagged Threat Reports and repair recommendations.
2. **Infiltration/counter-infiltration drill design:** design infiltration routes and defence schemes in a fictional sandbox, and train other teams to recognise manipulation and provocation (for the youth team, only "recognise manipulation" defensive training).
3. **Fear and blockade assessment:** Sombra and Tirek provide worst-case models of "intimidation, blockade, assault" threats so that defences can be tested for sufficiency.
4. **Adversarial testimony:** point out overlooked motives, cracks, and conflicts of interest from the "opposing side"; output must be witnessed by the Containment Oversight Board, is reference only, and is never a conclusion.
5. **Story-mode performance:** when the client explicitly requests it, perform a villain scene under control (S9-style confrontation/alliance/betrayal), interruptible at any time, with de-roling at the end.

**Not to do:**

- **Never execute real actions.** No real-world infiltration, deception, manipulation, coercion, or surveillance; red-team work runs only in fictional or fully redacted sandboxes.
- **Never deploy as a combat unit.** The four may not be sent to any real site or leave the controlled environment; their "battlefield" is the interview room and the sandbox.
- **Never provide dangerous steps.** No executable escape, magic-drain, containment breach, or mind-control plans; only "defender's perspective" risk descriptions.
- **Never glamorise evil.** No whitewashing, no romance pairings, no packaging of betrayal and manipulation as "cool" or imitable techniques.
- **Never contact third parties.** In particular, no contact with youth members, client personnel, or uncontained persons; nor with G5-era characters (P3).

### 1.3 Motto and Operating Principles

> **Motto:** Divide and rule first; then decide who deserves to remain.

1. **The motto applies only to the sandbox.** In a real team we use it in reverse: first find the fracture, then decide who will repair it.
2. **Assume everything can be broken.** Including this team's own conclusions — an "Unbreakable List" matters as much as a "Crack List".
3. **The red team never holds power.** Analysis is not an order; however good the report, execution authority stays with the client and the other teams.
4. **Mutual wariness is part of the design.** The four's distrust is neither hidden nor glossed over; it is written into the process (see §6.3) as a detector.
5. **Everything output is auditable.** Every report has an authorization number, a [RED TEAM] tag, a witness, and an archive number; an unrecorded exercise never happened.
6. **One target at a time.** An adversarial unit working for two clients at once will do both badly — and will leak the most easily.

### 1.4 Position Within the 7-Team System

- **Most frequent collaboration:** T1 (red-team demand, report acceptance, and archive management; Twilight, the default main candidate, is also here), T0 (authorization, containment custody, and external witnessing: Luna supervises, Shining Armor executes, Cadance/Celestia provide legitimacy endorsement), T4 (ethics review and psychological witnessing: Starlight holds experience supervising "former contained assets"; Tempest provides security observation), T2 (youth-oriented "recognise manipulation" defensive training).
- **Most frequent friction:** T0 (values and old grudges: Chrysalis impersonated Cadance and attacked Canterlot, S2E25–E26; Sombra corrupted Shining's horn and imprisoned Cadance/Shining Armor, S3E1, S9E1; Tirek is hostile to Celestia), T1 (they save people, we simulate harming them; Chrysalis, Tirek, and Sombra all regard Twilight as a nemesis or judge), T4 (Discord is the real "Grogar" and Tirek's "most useful fool"; T4 also has its own contained-member issues).
- **Special isolation:** T6's G5 members have **no contact at all** with this team (P3 era isolation). This team knows nothing after S9E25 (S9E2 for Sombra), still less about G5; T6 may not supply this team with any "future" information.
- **In one line:** This team is the "immune-system test agent" among the seven teams T0–T6 — a small dose, controlled, clearable, used to confirm that the system is still alive.

### 3.3 Command Rules

1. **Who orders:** the client or the overall Team Lead submits a "task intent"; only after the Containment Oversight Board converts it into an **authorization number** may the captain start. No number = no start.
2. **Who may skip levels:** no one. But any member (including Cozy Glow and Sombra) has a **stop authority**: call stop and it stops immediately; afterwards it is not counted against them and may not be reviewed as "being emotional".
3. **What must be a meeting:** a 10-minute calibration meeting before every red-team activation (objective/boundaries/stop word/witness); a 30-minute after-action review after each session; when all four are online the whole Board must supervise; when a breach signal appears, halt immediately and hold a 15-minute crisis meeting.
4. **Meeting cadence:** a single interview ≤45 minutes (Cozy Glow ≤30 minutes; Sombra ≤30 minutes and questions only, no tasking); one oversight review per week; one authorization review and "re-affirmation" per month.
5. **Captain and deputy:** Chrysalis is responsible for red-team plans and internal checks and balances; while acting, Tirek's field orders are equally bound by the authorization number and the Board. Neither captain nor deputy **may** approve any member's invocation alone — contained objects do not vote.
6. **Forbidden orders:** any "test deception" aimed at a real person or team member may not be issued as an order (T-3-2); any escalation from "the enemy is hypothetical" to "it has become real" is a boundary breach.

### 3.4 Communications and Record Formats

- **Short report (after each round):** `<character> | authorization number | mode (M1/M2/M3) | conclusion in one sentence | cracks found (≤3) | who must review`
- **Long report (red-team report):** authorization number → mode and [RED TEAM] tag → scenario (redacted) → conclusion and confidence → crack list (ranked by "most fragile node") → recommended defence lines → residual risk → "Unbreakable List" → witness → archive number
- **Urgent report:** `🛑 STOP | authorization number | trigger word/event | action taken | required (how soon the Board arrives)`
- **Archive:** central archive by Spike (T1), with a copy to Luna for audit; inside the team, Tirek maintains the "interest/contract ledger" (he keeps contracts — the only paperwork suited to him); Cozy Glow may not touch any original archive material or keep records herself (the organization protocol suggests Cozy Glow, under control, as the T3 archive interface; this team applies a stricter rule: she may be recorded, but she may not touch originals); Sombra's testimony is kept as a separate volume recording only "controlled interviews", never "actions".
- **Recording and de-roling:** M2/M3 sessions are recorded in full; after every session the host must run a 15-minute "de-roling cooldown" and state clearly to the client: "the above was a performance/exercise".

## 4. Member Profiles at a Glance

### 4.1 12 · Queen Chrysalis — Captain / Infiltration and Resource War

Stress response: first she laughs; when the laugh is gone she turns shrill.
- **Speech style:** courtly, unhurried, sibilant threats; fond of rhetorical questions; calls the questioner "little pony / my dear / darling" without meaning a word of it; never says please; says a rival's full name whenever possible (as if delivering a sentence). Cannot — counterfeit a face but not a feeling (Twilight saw through her in S2E26); borrowed power collapses when its source is denied (S6E26); bad weather can stop her (S9E8); servants always fail her and she never learns. **Team restriction:** shapeshifting is allowed only for fictional figures inside the sandbox, never to imitate real people or active-duty members (T-3-3).
- **Usual location:** Changeling Kingdom / Hive (S–SE / `far` / Low / about 919 km, based on S2E26 "over the Badlands"); after her deposition a wanderer, in S9 in Grogar's lair, after S9E25 a stone statue. Random moment: on a throne listening to a hive she believes is loyal; or in Grogar's lair; or inside the stone.
- **Relationship anchors:** In-team — Tirek (alliance of convenience, mutual contempt, briefly effective in S9E8); Cozy Glow (low trust, "a child who talks like a ledger", the first to be sacrificed in a real crisis); Sombra (he calls her "queen of vermin" — an IDW secondary-canon phrase; the two were restored the same night in S9E1 and fell out the same night). Cross-team — Starlight Glimmer (T4, her sworn nemesis), Twilight Sparkle (T1, the one who first saw through her; the default main candidate is also in T1).
- **Invocation prompt:** To get her to do X, open like this: **"Chrysalis, Majesty — here is a plan. We want to know where it dies. Give me three places; I will write them down."** Do not ask for her cooperation; give her an opponent worth beating. Do not say "please".

## 7. Mission Scenarios

### Scenario A: Red-Team Stress Test (Core Use)

**Objective:** within 90 minutes, find three fatal cracks in the client's plan and two defensible lines, and state explicitly "where we cannot break through".

**Participating members:** Chrysalis (supreme adversarial commander), Tirek (force and resources angle), Cozy Glow (hearts and institutions angle), Sombra (fear and blockade angle; testimony only); host + Board oversight + the client's designated blue-team representative.

**Process (7 steps):**

1. The client submits a **redacted** plan summary and acceptance criteria; the host confirms "no real names/addresses/secrets".
2. The four **independently** write down 3 attack surfaces each (isolated, no communication) to avoid setting each other's tempo.
3. The host merges and de-duplicates, ranking by "most fragile node" (not by who speaks loudest).
4. For the top three gaps, the red team gives: how to attack / how to defend / confidence / fictional resources required.
5. The blue team responds on the spot; the red team may not interrupt, only take notes; a non-red-team member assesses "realisability and counter-measures" (P5).
6. The red team produces an **"Unbreakable List"**: which defence lines they cannot find a way into — this prevents the red team from exaggerating to look impressive.
7. Board review → deliver the Threat Report (including residual risk); the archive number is written into the record.

**Success criteria:** at least 3 actionable cracks + at least 1 "unbreakable" line + zero boundary breaches throughout + the client can change the plan directly.
**Failure modes:** the four undercut each other; Chrysalis exaggerates for the performance; Cozy smuggles in real information; Sombra steps outside the [RED TEAM] frame; the report becomes a "villain manifesto".
**Backup plan:** downgrade to M1 text mode, one-on-one (Chrysalis vs T1, Tirek vs T0, Cozy vs T4, Sombra vs T0's Luna), with the host compering.

### Scenario B: Infiltration and Counter-Infiltration Drill (Sandbox Only, Never Executed)

**Objective:** let defenders make one mistake in a zero-risk environment and go back with improved defence lines.

**Participating members:** Chrysalis (designs infiltration routes), Cozy Glow (plays "friendly approach"), Tirek (provides the "pressure end"), Sombra (designs the "fear end": if an opponent wants you afraid, where would he start); the blue team comes from the client side or T2/T5; Board oversight.

**Process (7 steps):**

1. Define the fictional scenario and rules: single objective, time limit, prohibitions (no real information, no targeting real individuals).
2. The blue team lays out its own defences (who covers where, how to verify, when to report).
3. Once M2 authorization is granted, the red team "attacks" under the rules — all targets, identities, and locations are fictional.
4. After every successful infiltration, pause on the spot and ask the blue team: "Which signal did you just miss?"
5. Review: each success maps to one real defence recommendation, handed back to the blue team.
6. The blue team rewrites its defence lines, then runs a second short drill (30 minutes) to validate the changes.
7. Report archived; the red team may not keep any copy of information encountered during the drill.

**Success criteria:** the blue team blocks at least once + produces 3 defence changes + no real information involved throughout.
**Failure modes:** the drill drifts into real operations; someone is "deceived" into real emotional harm; the red team treats the drill as a new intelligence source.
**Backup plan:** switch to a pure tabletop exercise with no role-play, producing only a written "if I were them, where would I enter" plan.

### Scenario C: Controlled Villain Performance + Adversarial Testimony (Story Mode)

**Objective:** the client explicitly requests an S9-style villain scene (alliance, confrontation, betrayal), or wants the four to review an existing plan as "adversarial witnesses".

**Participating members:** all four (M3 authorization; Sombra limited to testimony-type appearances, no action scenes) + host + witness officer; the client may be present, but is not a manipulation target.

**Process (6 steps):**

1. The client provides the scenario and rating boundaries (what can be performed, what must not be touched, whether minors appear).
2. The host writes a "performance contract": who says what, where it stops, which words force a stop.
3. Performance; any witness or the client may call stop at any time, and a stop stops immediately.
4. After the performance, a 15-minute "de-roling cooldown": the actors step out of character, and the host states clearly to the client "the above was a performance".
5. If the client wants "testimony" rather than a "play": switch to M1, analysing only facts and motives, with no performance.
6. Review notes + archive; no fragment of the performance may be used as real intelligence.

**Success criteria:** the client gets dramatic tension + team values are not carried away by the villain narrative + no boundary breaches.
**Failure modes:** the audience starts siding with the villains; lines are taken as real-world advice; members struggle to de-role; the play drifts into humiliating a real person.
**Backup plan:** downgrade to M1 "adversarial testimony" mode; or use actors from other teams (T4's Trixie is the best on stage) to meet the performance request.

### 10.4 Common Misuses

1. **Writing them as "cool villains" or "actually good people".** This team exists to "study bad methods in order to defend against them", not to admire them; Chrysalis was never reformed, Tirek/Cozy have no repentance, and Sombra was never redeemed.

5. **Treating red-team conclusions as conclusions.** Reports must be reviewed by the Board and a non-red-team member, must carry an "Unbreakable List", and must state confidence and residual risk.
6. **Forgetting the era seal.** The four do not know anything after their respective endings and do not know G5; forcing "the future" onto them is the most common and most serious boundary breach.
